| Experiment | Question motivating the simulations | Simulation conditions | Planned measurements |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spontaneous emergence and cultural development** | What ideas, expressions, explanations, and practices develop in ordinary runs? Which remain individual, and which circulate? | Independent repetitions of the existing world, with the resolved configuration recorded. Include explicitly stable conditions where useful for comparison. | First appearances, later reuse, breadth of circulation, persistence, changing interpretations, and the composition of the developing cultural repertoire. |
| **Memory and transformation** | How does remembering alter what is transmitted? | Cross existing encoding-noise settings with verbatim-retention settings. Hold other mechanisms consistent across compared conditions. | Preservation of wording versus meaning, transformations between related uses, persistence through paraphrase, and associations between encoded memories and later expression. |
| **Reflection, abstraction, and recombination** | How do experiences become broader ideas? How are existing ideas connected or combined? | Cross reminding and reflection settings. Preserve the same world configuration across comparisons. | Connections between experiences and subsequent ideas, use across different contexts, formation of distinctions, combinations of earlier ideas, and movement from private representation to public expression. |
| **Social structure and local cultures** | How do interaction patterns influence shared meanings and group differences? | Vary existing cross-group relationship and bridge settings, while specifying the remaining topology and routine settings consistently. | Within-group similarities, between-group differences, movement across groups, reinterpretation after crossing, and relationships between actual exposure networks and cultural development. |
| **Communication and negotiation** | How do different communication opportunities influence the development of understanding? | Compare combinations of clarification, handover, and meeting mechanisms, using explicit schedules. | Clarification and negotiation episodes, immediate repetition, later reuse, expressed agreement, competing interpretations, and subsequent persistence. |
| **Speech, records, and cultural memory** | How do ideas change as they move between conversation, memory, and shared writing? | Compare existing binder access settings, such as hidden contents, current contents, and available history. Record-writing settings remain explicit. | Speech-to-record and record-to-speech movement, condensation, reinterpretation, competing formulations, longevity, and dependence on particular sources. |
| **Turnover and inheritance** | What do newcomers inherit, reinterpret, abandon, or introduce? | Compare no turnover, one replacement wave, and two waves within the existing population capacity. Combine with record-access conditions where the question calls for it. | Acquisition histories, cohort differences, survival after departures, new interpretations, and changes attributable to newcomers versus continuing members. |
| **Changing circumstances and historical dependence** | How does culture respond to changes in the world? What remains when circumstances change again? | Compare stable conditions, a regime change, and change followed by reversion; include appropriate visible-cue comparisons. | Changes in explanations, categories, implications, and usage; persistence of earlier interpretations; recovery or replacement after reversion; differences associated with prior history. |
| **Influence, competition, and coexistence** | Why do some ideas or interpretations become more prevalent? When do alternatives coexist? | Compare existing prestige settings and conformity settings, including reassigned prestige scores where useful. | Uptake, source concentration, relative prevalence of alternatives, replacement, coexistence, and relationships between influence and exposure. |
| **Social and pragmatic meaning** | How do ideas acquire functions such as humor, criticism, warning, affiliation, or obligation? | Compare existing social-reward and emotion settings, while also examining naturally occurring audience and context differences. | Changes in communicative function, evaluation, stance, group association, and relationships between those changes and subsequent circulation. |
| **Contingency and recurring patterns** | How much cultural development is specific to a particular society? What recurs across societies? | Repeat societies under matched world settings, vary world realizations, and compare available agent models with a consistent observer. | Recurring ideas, different expressions of analogous concepts, society-specific developments, and variation across world, agent, and model realizations. |
| **Long-horizon cultural development** | Does the society accumulate ideas, stabilize, fragment, cycle, or repeatedly forget? | Run extended histories and analyze their trajectories at multiple points. Preserve the dependence between observations from the same run. | Changes in repertoire, persistence, recurrence, differentiation, combinations of ideas, and the relationship between individual memory and collective continuity. |
